
# Short ispace Meeting Guide

## Opening

> ANUMA converts lunar mission requirements into constraints, ranked candidate regions, and explainable recommendations. Our MAGPIE-inspired demo uses public data and synthetic assumptions. We are not requesting confidential mission information.

## 12 Questions

1. Where does landing-region analysis currently require the most time or manual work?
2. Which part could ANUMA realistically improve without replacing existing engineering tools?
3. Who would use it, and who would review or approve its results?
4. For a synthetic mission, which criteria should be hard constraints and which should be preferences?
5. Which public datasets should we use?
6. What outputs are most useful: feasible regions, rankings, exclusion reasons, reports, or GIS files?
7. What evidence would make ispace trust—or reject—the methodology?
8. How should ANUMA present uncertainty, missing data, and unstable rankings?
9. What claims should we avoid because they overstate the prototype?
10. Would a public-data, non-confidential pilot be useful?
11. What would be the smallest valuable pilot and its success metric?
12. What single change would most improve our NeoSpace demonstration?

## Close With

- What did we misunderstand?
- May we reference this discussion or feedback? If so, exactly how?
- Who owns the next action, and what should we send them?

## Avoid Asking

- Exact landing location, ellipse, navigation accuracy, slope limit, propulsion margin, internal datasets, customer requirements, or internal rankings.
- Ask instead for public ranges, synthetic examples, publications, and qualitative feedback.

## 30-Minute Agenda

- **3 min:** introductions and confidentiality boundary
- **5 min:** ANUMA demonstration
- **10 min:** workflow, criteria, and data
- **7 min:** validation, outputs, and pilot
- **5 min:** corrections, permissions, and next steps
