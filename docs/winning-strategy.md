
# Winning Strategy

## Executive Summary

ANUMA wins if judges believe three things at once:

1. a real mission team has a painful, costly decision problem;
2. ANUMA produces a defensible answer faster than today’s manual workflow; and
3. the team understands exactly where the answer stops being trustworthy.

The winning entry is a narrow, working evidence chain:

> “For this lander, window, footprint, and science objective, `[computed result]` of the study area fails or remains unknown. These `[computed count]` regions remain viable. The recommendation changes under `[computed sensitivity result]`. Here are the data, assumptions, support scales, confidence, and exportable trade record.”

That is more credible than “AI finds the best place on the Moon.”

## Judge Evaluation

| Dimension | What earns a high score | What would weaken ANUMA |
|---|---|---|
| Innovation | Requirements compile into constraints and scoring; robustness and provenance are first-class | Generic layer overlay, chatbot, or map viewer |
| Technical complexity | Correct lunar geospatial pipeline, time-dependent layer, feasible-region extraction, sensitivity analysis | Many technologies with no end-to-end proof |
| Scientific credibility | Scale-aware methods, uncertainty, authoritative data, published comparison, limitations | Unqualified “safe” or “water-rich” claims |
| Feasibility | One polished vertical slice with deterministic results and tests | Global, real-time, all-mission ambition |
| Presentation value | Visible cause-and-effect and a clear decision narrative | Long feature tour or dense 3D globe |
| Economic value | Named user, workflow integration, measurable analyst-time reduction | Vague “space agencies” market |
| Regulatory maturity | Provenance, licensing, liability boundaries, responsible-use framing | Generic space-law slide disconnected from product |

No detailed official scoring rubric has been published. The official programme nevertheless requires **technical, regulatory, and economic** treatment, so omitting either nontechnical dimension is a strategic error.

## Risks

- A visually strong map may invite questions the prototype cannot answer.
- Claiming superiority to NASA tools is neither credible nor necessary.
- Reproducing known Artemis regions can look hard-coded.
- “Explainable AI” language may trigger scrutiny without training data or validated labels.
- HPC can look like solutionism before a proven computational bottleneck.
- A market slide based on the number of lunar missions is not a business model.
- Judges may ask how requirements were chosen and whether rankings change with weights.

## Better Alternatives

### Winning scope

Use one reference scenario and three deliberate comparisons:

1. **Balanced mission:** safety, power/illumination, communications, and science access.
2. **Safety-first variant:** tighter slope and ellipse-buffer constraints.
3. **Science-access variant:** stronger preference for proximity to a declared PSR or other observable. Access, neutron suppression, radar response, or thermal conditions must not be equated with extractable water.

The same pipeline should produce visibly different but explainable results. Add a “weight stress test” rather than a second planet, 3D mode, or generative assistant.

### Defensible innovation claims

- “Mission requirements become executable geospatial policy.”
- “Rankings include robustness and data adequacy, not just a weighted mean.”
- “Every recommendation carries a reproducible evidence manifest.”
- “ANUMA bridges public planetary data and early mission trade studies.”

ANUMA’s novelty is workflow and evidence integration—not invention of new lunar measurements or higher-fidelity physics.

### Business wedge

Offer ANUMA initially as:

- a paid collaborative study workspace for mission concept teams;
- curated lunar data and reproducible scenario templates;
- expert-assisted site-screening reports;
- later, private deployment and APIs for operators.

Do not sell “landing safety certification.” The initial value metric is analyst weeks avoided and faster scenario turnover.

## Missing Components

To be competition-ready, ANUMA needs:

- a one-sentence target user and job-to-be-done;
- five documented customer/problem interviews;
- one reference mission requirements document;
- a criterion registry with equations, units, thresholds, direction, source, and uncertainty;
- a side-by-side scenario comparison;
- rank-stability/sensitivity output;
- a published-region benchmark plus at least one disagreement analysis;
- a data/provenance panel;
- a regulatory/liability slide tied to actual product controls;
- a pricing and delivery hypothesis;
- a 90-second fallback video or deterministic offline demo;
- an explicit “screening, not certification” boundary.

## NeoSpace Impact

### The answer to “What would make this project win?”

**A live, falsifiable decision—not a product tour.**

The judges should watch ANUMA reject infeasible terrain, produce candidate regions, recommend one, expose uncertainty, and then revise its recommendation when a mission requirement changes. The presenter should compare the result with a published NASA candidate region and explain both agreement and limitations.

The final pitch should resolve:

- **Why now:** lunar mission cadence and south-polar operations increase trade complexity.
- **Why ANUMA:** existing tools expose data; ANUMA operationalizes requirements and captures evidence.
- **Why this team:** it demonstrates geospatial competence and scientific restraint.
- **Why Luxembourg:** direct relevance to its space ecosystem, data/HPC capabilities, and mission-operations companies.
- **Why a business:** reusable workflow and scenario collaboration, not one-off cartography.

## Scientific Credibility Assessment

Judges should be able to inspect:

- source product and version;
- analysis CRS and resolution;
- criterion formula and threshold;
- nodata/coverage treatment;
- uncertainty/confidence;
- date/window assumptions;
- sensitivity of the result;
- known unmodelled factors.

The presentation should explicitly say:

> “ANUMA ranks regions supported by the available evidence. It does not certify touchdown safety, infer subsurface resources as fact, or replace trajectory and lander-specific engineering.”

This limitation increases trust.

## Recommended Next Action

Create a one-page “win contract” and reject every feature that does not support it:

- live run completes reliably;
- at least three contiguous feasible candidates;
- recommendation changes logically under one requirement change;
- sensitivity is visible;
- one candidate overlaps or meaningfully compares with a published region;
- every displayed value has provenance;
- no quantitative Artemis overlap without defensible geometry, no mission-window illumination claim from average visibility, and no local resource ranking from a coarse hydrogen grid;
- the pitch covers technical, regulatory, and economic dimensions in equal credibility.
